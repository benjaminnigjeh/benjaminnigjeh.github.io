## Selected Research in AI and Proteomics

The three research manuscripts below trace one line of work from general-purpose
unsupervised representation learning of proteomic data, to a supervised classifier
applied to a specific clinical question, to a self-supervised pipeline that combines
both ends into an automated labeling workflow. None of these manuscripts should be
described as peer-reviewed or formally published unless that status is confirmed —
they are presented here as research manuscripts / selected works.

  <span class="status-badge">Research Manuscript</span>
  <h3 id="research-1-title">Variational Autoencoders for Quantitative Proteomic Data Compression and Inference</h3>
  <p class="research-context">Manuscript context: <em>Bioinformatics</em></p>
  <p class="research-source-title">Source manuscript title: “Variable Autoencoders in the Unsupervised Data Compression and Inference from Discovery-based Quantitative Proteomic Datasets”</p>

  <p>
    Developed a fully connected variational autoencoder for nonlinear dimensionality
    reduction, visualization, clustering, and inference from high-dimensional
    quantitative proteomic datasets. The model compressed thousands of protein
    measurements into a two-dimensional latent representation and was evaluated using
    synthetic datasets, TMT pancreatic tissue proteomics, and multi-study Super-SILAC
    breast cancer proteomics.
  </p>

  <ul class="research-highlights">
    <li>10-plex TMT pancreatic tissue dataset — 54 samples, 7,699 quantified proteins</li>
    <li>Unsupervised separation of pancreatic cancer tissue from healthy controls</li>
    <li>Successful latent-space inference of unseen test cancer samples</li>
    <li>Super-SILAC breast tissue analysis — 242 samples, 6,703 quantified proteins</li>
    <li>Combined dataset spanned three separately published studies</li>
    <li>Study-specific clustering demonstrated the impact of experimental design and batch effects</li>
  </ul>

  <ul class="tech-tags" aria-label="Technologies used">
    <li>Variational Autoencoder</li>
    <li>Unsupervised Learning</li>
    <li>TensorFlow</li>
    <li>Keras</li>
    <li>TMT Proteomics</li>
    <li>Super-SILAC</li>
    <li>Latent-Space Inference</li>
    <li>Dimensionality Reduction</li>
  </ul>

  <details class="tech-details">
    <summary>Technical details</summary>
    <p>
      A fully connected encoder/decoder was benchmarked against a convolutional
      variant and selected for training speed and comparable clustering quality
      (k-means silhouette score). Training behavior was first characterized on
      synthetic and partial MNIST datasets — measuring Euclidean distance between
      latent-space cluster centers — to study how data quantity and feature quality
      affect compression before applying the model to the pancreatic and breast
      tissue proteomic datasets.
    </p>
  </details>

<article class="research-card" aria-labelledby="research-2-title">
  <span class="status-badge">Research Manuscript</span>
  <h3 id="research-2-title">Machine Learning for Early-Stage Pancreatic Cancer Detection from Plasma Proteomics</h3>
  <p class="research-context">Manuscript context: <em>PROTEOMICS</em></p>
  <p class="research-source-title">Source manuscript title: “Data-Independent Acquisition Mass Spectrometry and Machine Learning Algorithms in the Early-Stage Diagnosis of Pancreatic Cancer from Clinical Plasma Samples”</p>

  <p>
    Developed and benchmarked neural-network and classical machine-learning models
    for classification of early-stage pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma using
    quantitative plasma proteomics generated by data-independent acquisition mass
    spectrometry.
  </p>

  <ul class="research-highlights">
    <li>38 total clinical plasma samples — 19 early-stage pancreatic cancer vs. 19 healthy controls</li>
    <li>1,898 quantified plasma proteins across 10,871 unique peptides</li>
    <li>Balanced 28-sample training cohort with an independent 10-sample hold-out test set</li>
    <li>Neural network optimized with k-fold and leave-one-out cross-validation plus early-stopping regularization</li>
    <li>Benchmarked against logistic regression, random forest, and multinomial naïve Bayes</li>
    <li>58 differentially quantified proteins, associated mainly with immune-response and metabolic processes</li>
  </ul>

  <ul class="tech-tags" aria-label="Technologies used">
    <li>DIA-MS</li>
    <li>Clinical Proteomics</li>
    <li>Neural Networks</li>
    <li>Machine Learning</li>
    <li>Skyline</li>
    <li>Cross-Validation</li>
    <li>ROC Analysis</li>
    <li>Pancreatic Cancer</li>
  </ul>

  <details class="tech-details">
    <summary>Technical details</summary>
    <p>
      Network architecture (node counts per hidden layer) was tuned via a keras-tuner
      k-fold search on the training cohort. Training length was set using a
      leave-one-out evaluation-loss curve, and prediction accuracy on the hold-out
      test cohort was compared across activation functions (ReLU, ELU, SELU, Tanh)
      and optimizers (Adam, Nadam, Adamax, RMSProp, SGD, Adagrad, Adadelta, Ftrl)
      before benchmarking against classical machine-learning baselines using ROC/AUC.
    </p>
  </details>

<article class="research-card" aria-labelledby="research-3-title">
  <span class="status-badge">Research Manuscript</span>
  <h3 id="research-3-title">Self-Supervised Proteomics with VAE-Based Compression and Density-Based Clustering</h3>
  <p class="research-context">Manuscript context: <em>Analytical Chemistry</em></p>
  <p class="research-source-title">Source manuscript title: “Fully Connected Self-Supervised Deep Learners in Data-Driven Mass Spectrometry Proteomics”</p>

  <p>
    Designed a self-supervised proteomics workflow that combines a fully connected
    variational autoencoder with density-based clustering to compress proteomic
    datasets, discover sample groups, generate labels, and support downstream
    supervised classifiers.
  </p>

  <ul class="research-highlights">
    <li>Full pipeline: mzML conversion → Comet database search → PeptideProphet / Trans-Proteomic Pipeline → Skyline quantification</li>
    <li>Fully connected variational autoencoder for 2-D latent-space dimensionality reduction</li>
    <li>DBSCAN-based automated clustering, benchmarked against k-means, agglomerative, spectral clustering, and affinity propagation</li>
    <li>Evaluated across 4 proteomic sample types: whole-tissue lysates, SCX fractions, immunoprecipitated samples, excised gel bands</li>
    <li>DBSCAN epsilon hyperparameter optimized using homogeneity, completeness, and V-measure</li>
    <li>Labeled tuning samples used to calibrate clustering ahead of downstream supervised classifiers</li>
  </ul>

  <ul class="tech-tags" aria-label="Technologies used">
    <li>Self-Supervised Learning</li>
    <li>VAE</li>
    <li>DBSCAN</li>
    <li>Comet</li>
    <li>Trans-Proteomic Pipeline</li>
    <li>Skyline</li>
    <li>mzML</li>
    <li>Automated Labeling</li>
  </ul>

  <details class="tech-details">
    <summary>Technical details</summary>
    <p>
      DBSCAN's epsilon parameter was swept and scored by homogeneity, completeness,
      and their harmonic mean (V-measure) on synthetic noise datasets and on four
      real proteomic sample-type groups, establishing a tune-sample-based calibration
      strategy for automated cluster labeling ahead of downstream supervised
      classifiers such as fully connected neural network classifiers.
    </p>
  </details>
