Concepts behind the work

A quick primer on the AI/ML building blocks referenced throughout this site.

Large Language Models (LLMs)

Transformer-based models trained on large text corpora to predict and generate language, increasingly used as reasoning and orchestration engines for scientific and agentic workflows rather than just chat interfaces.

Agentic AI

Systems where an LLM (or a coordinated set of them) plans, calls tools, and takes multi-step actions toward a goal with limited human intervention — the pattern behind supervisor/expert-agent architectures like ProteoAgent.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Grounds a model's output in retrieved documents or data rather than parametric memory alone — reduces hallucination and lets a system reason over up-to-date or domain-specific scientific knowledge.

Protein Foundation Models

Large models pretrained on protein sequence/structure data (protein language models) that produce transferable embeddings useful for downstream tasks — identification, function prediction, and structure inference.

Contrastive Learning

Trains a model to pull representations of similar examples together and push dissimilar ones apart, without requiring dense labels — well suited to matching spectra to candidate identifications.

Self-Supervised Learning

Generates its own training signal from unlabeled data (e.g., via reconstruction or clustering), then uses the resulting representations or pseudo-labels to bootstrap downstream supervised models.

Transformers

The attention-based architecture underlying modern LLMs and protein language models — models relationships between all elements of a sequence in parallel rather than step by step.

Graph Neural Networks

Learn over graph-structured data by passing and aggregating information between connected nodes — a natural fit for evidence that spans consecutive MS1 scans, precursor ions, and MS/MS identifications.
